<h2 id="cost">What Hiring on Fiverr Really Costs</h2>

<p>The price on a gig is not the price you pay. Fiverr adds a service fee at checkout. According to Fiverr's Help Center and its Payment Terms, the fee is <strong>5.5% of the purchase amount</strong>, plus a <strong>$3.50 small order fee for purchases under $200</strong>. Sales tax or VAT may also apply, depending on where you live.</p>

<h3>Three money tips</h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>Bundle small jobs.</strong> Five $20 orders cost $17.50 in small order fees alone. One $100 custom offer costs only one.</li>
<li><strong>Extras and tips have their own fee.</strong> Every separate payment carries the service fee, so add extras at checkout when you can.</li>
<li><strong>Ask for a custom offer</strong> if your job doesn't fit a package. It's often cheaper than piling on extras.</li>
</ul>

<h2 id="steps">How to Hire on Fiverr: 7 Steps</h2>

<p>Here is the full process from first search to final file.</p>

<ol>
<li><strong>Define the job in one sentence.</strong> For example: "A 30-second vertical promo video for my bakery's Instagram."</li>
<li><strong>Search with specific words.</strong> "Bakery Instagram reel" beats "video editing." Use filters for budget, delivery time and seller details.</li>

<li><strong>Order with a clear brief</strong> and attach examples.</li>
<li><strong>Review the delivery within three days.</strong> Accept, or request a revision with specific notes.</li>

<h2 id="vet">How to Vet a Seller in 10 Minutes</h2>

<p>Star ratings on Fiverr are almost always high, so they don't separate good from great. Look deeper. Here's what to check and why.</p>

<div class="pr-table">
<table>
<thead><tr><th>What to check</th><th>Good sign</th><th>Warning sign</th></tr></thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td>Portfolio</td><td>Work that looks like your project</td><td>Only stock images or generic mockups</td></tr>
<tr><td>Recent reviews</td><td>Detailed reviews from the last 3 months</td><td>Many one-word reviews, or a long silence</td></tr>
<tr><td>Review prices</td><td>Reviews from buyers who paid similar amounts</td><td>All reviews from $5 orders, but you're spending $300</td></tr>
<tr><td>Gig description</td><td>Clear scope: what's included and what's not</td><td>Vague promises like "guaranteed results"</td></tr>
<tr><td>Response to your message</td><td>Asks smart questions about your goal</td><td>Copy-paste reply, or pushes you to buy at once</td></tr>
<tr><td>Revisions and files</td><td>States revision count and file formats</td><td>Silent on source files or usage rights</td></tr>
</tbody>
</table>
</div>

<h3>The one-question test</h3>

<p>Before ordering, send a short message like: "I need X for Y audience. What would you need from me, and what would you do differently from your standard package?" A strong seller answers your actual situation. A weak one sends a template.</p>

<h2 id="brief">Write a Brief That Gets Great Work</h2>

<p>Most disputes start with a vague brief. If the seller has to guess, you'll probably be disappointed. Use this simple template.</p>

<div class="pr-table">
<table>
<thead><tr><th>Brief section</th><th>What to write</th><th>Example</th></tr></thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td>Goal</td><td>What the work must achieve</td><td>"Get more clicks on our summer sale ad"</td></tr>
<tr><td>Audience</td><td>Who will see it</td><td>"Busy parents, 30–45, on mobile"</td></tr>
<tr><td>Deliverables</td><td>Exact files, sizes, lengths</td><td>"3 versions, 1080×1920, MP4 + project file"</td></tr>
<tr><td>Examples</td><td>2–3 things you like, and why</td><td>"This ad's pacing; that one's colors"</td></tr>

<tr><td>Deadline</td><td>Real date, with a buffer</td><td>"Need by the 15th; launch is the 20th"</td></tr>
</tbody>
</table>
</div>

<h2 id="protect">Your Buyer Protection: The Timelines</h2>

<p>Fiverr holds your payment until the order completes. That gives you real leverage, but only if you know the clock. Here are the key timelines from Fiverr's Help Center.</p>

<div class="pr-table">
<table>
<thead><tr><th>Situation</th><th>What you can do</th><th>Time limit</th></tr></thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td>Seller delivers</td><td>Accept, request a revision, or ask for more time to review</td><td>Order auto-completes after <strong>3 days</strong> with no action (14 days for shipped items)</td></tr>
<tr><td>Order is late</td><td>Cancel through the Resolution Center without seller approval</td><td><strong>24 hours</strong> after the expected delivery time</td></tr>
<tr><td>You request a cancellation</td><td>Seller can accept or decline</td><td>Seller has <strong>48 hours</strong>; no reply means it's canceled</td></tr>
<tr><td>Order is canceled</td><td>Money goes to your Fiverr balance; you can request it back to your card or PayPal</td><td>Refunds to the original method usually take up to <strong>10 days</strong></td></tr>
</tbody>
</table>
</div>

<div class="pr-box pr-warn">
<strong>Don't miss the 3-day window.</strong> The Resolution Center is not available for completed orders. If you're busy, open the delivery the same day and at least request more time. Once it auto-completes, your options shrink a lot.
</div>

<h3>How to ask for a good revision</h3>
<ul class="pr-check">
<li>✅ Point to the exact part: "At 0:12, the logo is cut off."</li>
<li>✅ Refer back to your brief: "We asked for 1080×1920; this is 1080×1080."</li>
<li>✅ Stay polite and specific. Sellers fix clear notes fast.</li>
<li>❌ Don't add new scope as a "revision." Ask for a custom offer instead.</li>
</ul>

<h2 id="flags">Red Flags and Scams to Avoid</h2>

<p>Fiverr is a legitimate marketplace, but like any open platform it has bad actors. Walk away if you see any of these.</p>

<ul>
<li><strong>"Let's continue on WhatsApp or email."</strong> Fiverr's Payment Terms forbid paying outside the site, and you lose all protection if you do.</li>
<li><strong>Promises that sound too good.</strong> "Page 1 on Google in 7 days" or "10,000 real followers for $10" are not realistic.</li>
<li><strong>Requests for your passwords.</strong> Give limited access, like a collaborator role, never your main login.</li>
<li><strong>Portfolio images found elsewhere.</strong> A quick reverse image search can expose stolen work.</li>
<li><strong>Pressure to accept before you've checked.</strong> Take your time inside the 3-day window.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Five Mistakes First-Time Buyers Make</h2>

<ol>
<li><strong>Choosing the cheapest gig by default.</strong> A $5 gig can be fine for a tiny task. For work that represents your brand, low price often means rushed work or templates.</li>
<li><strong>Skipping the message.</strong> Ordering blind is the fastest way to a mismatch. One question first saves days later.</li>
<li><strong>Using a vague brief.</strong> "Make it pop" means something different to everyone. Show examples instead.</li>
<li><strong>Ignoring delivery time.</strong> Leave a buffer of a few days before your real deadline. Even good sellers get sick or busy.</li>
<li><strong>Leaving a review in anger.</strong> If something is wrong, use revisions and the Resolution Center first. Reviews are for the final result, and fair feedback helps other buyers.</li>
</ol>

<h2 id="faq">Frequently Asked Questions</h2>

<h3>Is it safe to hire on Fiverr?</h3>
<p>Yes, if you stay on the platform. Fiverr holds your payment until the order completes and gives you a Resolution Center for problems. Most risk comes from paying off-site or from rushing past the 3-day review window.</p>

<h3>How much does Fiverr charge buyers?</h3>
<p>Fiverr adds a 5.5% service fee to each payment, plus a $3.50 small order fee on purchases under $200. Taxes may apply depending on your location.</p>

<h3>Can I get a refund on Fiverr?</h3>
<p>Yes, when an order is canceled. Canceled funds go to your Fiverr balance by default, and you can request a refund to your original payment method, which usually takes up to 10 days.</p>

<h3>What happens if a Fiverr seller is late?</h3>
<p>If the seller misses the expected delivery time and you didn't agree to an extension, you can cancel through the Resolution Center 24 hours later without the seller's approval.</p>

<h3>Should I tip on Fiverr?</h3>
<p>Tips are optional. Fiverr's minimum tip is $5, and tips also carry the service fee. Tip when a seller truly goes above the brief.</p>

<h3>Is Fiverr Pro worth it?</h3>
<p>For high-stakes work like a brand identity or a product launch, often yes, because sellers are vetted and eligible orders have a money-back guarantee. For small, simple tasks, regular Fiverr is usually fine.</p>

How to interpret your result

  • 0–39: Few recommended signals have been verified. Pause and investigate the missing information before paying or sharing data.
  • 40–69: Some useful signals are present. Check the remaining items and look for independent evidence.
  • 70–89: Many recommended signals are visible. Still verify the seller, terms and payment route independently.
  • 90–100: You have checked most of the list. This is strong checklist coverage, not a safety certification.

The score counts the points attached to items you personally confirmed. It does not grade the website against a hidden database. A high score cannot rule out malware, phishing, impersonation, fake reviews or a compromised site.

Further checks from trusted sources

  • US Federal Trade Commission: online shopping advice — review sellers, payment methods and refund terms.
  • Google Safe Browsing site status — check whether Google currently flags a site as dangerous. A clean result is not a guarantee.
  • ICANN Lookup — review available domain registration information. Domain age or privacy protection alone does not establish trust.
  • CISA Secure Our World — practical guidance for safer online accounts and browsing.

Frequently asked questions

What is a website trust score? +
It is a structured way to review visible signals such as clear ownership, contact details, policies, payment terms, independent reviews and evidence for claims. Different services use different methods, so a score is only meaningful when its method is explained.
Does this checklist scan a website or detect malware? +
No. It does not fetch a URL, inspect code, scan for malware or query a reputation database. You check the signals yourself; the score is calculated locally in your browser and is not uploaded.
Does HTTPS mean a website is trustworthy? +
No. HTTPS encrypts the connection, but scammers can also use HTTPS. Check the business identity, contact information, terms, payment route and independent evidence as well.
What should I check before paying a website? +
Confirm who operates it, how to contact them, the full price and renewal terms, refund and cancellation rules, privacy practices, independent reviews and whether payments stay within a protected checkout.
Can a high checklist score guarantee that a website is safe? +
No. A high score only means you confirmed many recommended trust signals. It cannot rule out fraud, malware, impersonation, compromised pages or misleading claims.

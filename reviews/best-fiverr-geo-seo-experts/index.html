Why GEO is a timely buyer category

Search behavior is changing: buyers increasingly ask AI assistants for comparisons and recommendations before visiting a brand's website. Fiverr's September 2026 Holiday Marketing Index reported that 67% of surveyed freelance search specialists saw rising retailer demand for GEO, compared with 56% reporting rising demand for traditional search rankings. That is a survey of specialists—not a measured ranking uplift or proof that GEO replaces SEO.

The commercial opportunity is clear: a business may already have useful products and strong reviews but lack the structured, accessible, consistent information that helps search systems understand what it offers. A capable consultant can audit those gaps. The risk is that GEO is a young market with vague packages, unverifiable “AI ranking” claims and sellers who re-label generic backlink bundles as AI optimization.

What a real GEO audit should contain

  • Entity clarity: consistent brand name, services, locations, founders and authoritative profiles across the website and reputable sources.
  • Technical accessibility: important pages crawlable, indexable, fast enough to use, internally linked and not blocked by accidental directives.
  • Content usefulness: direct answers, original evidence, clear authorship, update dates, transparent methodology and pages that solve specific buyer questions.
  • Structured data: valid Organization, Product, Article, Person or LocalBusiness markup only where it matches visible page content.
  • Source and citation review: a documented baseline of brand mentions and citations across selected AI/search experiences, with dates and exact prompts.
  • Measurement plan: separate organic-search traffic, referral sessions, branded searches and AI citations instead of claiming that a citation equals a sale.

Ask for a prioritized audit with URLs, screenshots or reproducible checks, impact estimates and a list of changes. “We will rank you in ChatGPT in seven days” is not a technical specification.

Questions to ask before ordering

  1. Which AI engines and query set will you monitor, and how will you record the baseline?
  2. Will you change my website, or deliver an audit and recommendations only?
  3. Can I see a redacted before-and-after report for a similar business?
  4. Do you use paid links, mass guest posts, fake reviews or automated brand mentions? If yes, decline.
  5. How will you protect access to my CMS, Search Console and analytics?
  6. What will be delivered at the end: changed URLs, schema tests, content briefs, citations log and a handover?

How much should you spend?

Fiverr listings range from small diagnostic gigs to multi-week SEO engagements. Do not compare the headline price alone. A low-cost report may be useful as a first diagnostic, but it is not equivalent to technical implementation, content research and ongoing measurement. Start with a fixed-scope audit; only fund implementation after reviewing the findings. Keep the first project small enough that you can judge the quality of the deliverables.

Verdict: buy evidence, not an AI-ranking promise

GEO is worth testing when your business depends on discovery and your website already has a solid product, useful content and clear brand information. Among the public Fiverr profiles reviewed, Miranda Davis and Manav S offer broader SEO backgrounds, while Fab SEO Expert presents a more focused GEO gig. The right choice depends on your CMS, market and whether you need implementation. Ask for a documented baseline and a transparent work log. Treat guaranteed citations, guaranteed rankings and fabricated AI mentions as red flags.

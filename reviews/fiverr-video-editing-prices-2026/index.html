Fiverr Video Editing Prices in 2026: What Should You Budget?

A video editor's starting price is only the first number. The final quote depends on footage length, finished runtime, captions, motion graphics, revisions, deadline and usage rights. This guide explains the price signals buyers can compare before ordering.

What do Fiverr video editors charge?

Fiverr's own guide to video-editor costs, published in August 2026, says a basic video-editing project averages about $65. It also notes that short social videos may be priced from around $10 per finished minute, while advanced work can cost more. Those figures are useful reference points, not a guaranteed quote: a one-minute edit with captions and simple cuts is not the same job as a polished ad with animation, sound design and multiple revisions.

The live Fiverr category displays a wide range of starting prices. At the time of this review, examples on the category page included offers starting at $5, $15, $20, $45 and higher. A “from” price usually describes the seller's entry package; check the package details before treating it as the price for your complete brief.

Why the cheapest package may not be the cheapest project

  • Raw footage limit: a low tier may accept only a few minutes of source video.
  • Finished runtime: clarify the final length and whether extra minutes cost more.
  • Captions and graphics: subtitles, branded lower thirds and animated text may be add-ons.
  • Revisions: check how many revision rounds are included and what counts as a revision.
  • Usage rights: ask whether music, stock assets and the finished edit are cleared for your intended commercial use.
  • Delivery: confirm the file format, aspect ratios, project files and deadline.

How to compare Fiverr editors before you order

  1. Write a one-paragraph brief with the platform, finished length, source-footage length and desired style.
  2. Open portfolio samples that resemble your actual project—not just the seller's most impressive montage.
  3. Compare the Basic, Standard and Premium packages line by line.
  4. Ask whether captions, licensed music, stock footage, source files and commercial usage are included.
  5. Confirm delivery time, revision limits and the process for sending large source files.
  6. Use a small paid test edit when the project is recurring or high-value.

Questions buyers ask

Is $5 enough for a video edit?

It may be enough for a very small, tightly scoped task or an entry package. It should not be assumed to cover a full-length edit, multiple versions, licensed assets and unlimited revisions.

Does a longer video always cost more?

Usually, longer source footage and a longer finished runtime add editing time, but complexity matters too. A short commercial with animation can require more work than a longer simple talking-head cut.

Should I send raw footage before ordering?

Send a short sample or ask the seller how they prefer to receive files. Agree on scope, privacy and delivery before sharing sensitive or unreleased material.
